⚡ Energy & Particle Model

  • Matter is modelled as made of tiny particles.
  • Particle motion is linked to kinetic energy.
  • Changing state changes how particles are arranged and how they move.
  • The recap materials distinguish kinetic energy from potential energy.
Recap: Energy and the Particle Model

🔥 Heat, Temperature & Conduction

  • Temperature is linked to the average kinetic energy of particles.
  • Heat transfer occurs when there is a temperature difference, from hotter matter to cooler matter.
  • Conduction transfers thermal energy through direct contact between particles.
  • Metals conduct well because their outer electrons are free to move and transfer energy.
  • Insulators are poor conductors; trapped air is useful because air is a poor conductor.
Heat, Temperature and Conduction

🌡️ Convection & Insulation

  • Convection occurs in liquids and gases as the heated substance moves.
  • Warm fluid becomes less dense and tends to rise.
  • Cooler, denser fluid tends to sink, creating convection currents.
  • The insulation investigation asks how insulation changes heat transfer to ice.
  • Variables, controls, repeat trials, averages, graphs and a conclusion are key parts of the investigation.
Convection + 2026 Insulation Investigation

☀️ Radiation

  • Radiation transfers heat using electromagnetic waves.
  • Infrared radiation can travel through a vacuum, which is how energy can travel from the Sun to Earth.
  • Dark surfaces are good absorbers and emitters in the supplied notes.
  • Shiny/reflective surfaces tend to reflect more infrared radiation.
Heat Transfer: Radiation

🔊 Sound & Waves

  • Sound starts with a vibration and travels through a medium.
  • Sound is treated as a mechanical longitudinal wave in the supplied lessons.
  • Compressions are crowded regions; rarefactions are spread-out regions.
  • Amplitude is linked to loudness; frequency is linked to pitch.
  • Sound travels faster through solids than gases because particles are closer together.
Introduction to Sound + Sound & Particles

🔌 Electricity & Circuits

  • Current is the flow of electrons around a connected circuit and is measured in amps.
  • Voltage provides the push on electrons and is measured in volts.
  • Ammeters measure current; voltmeters measure voltage.
  • In series, current is the same at every point and supply voltage is shared.
  • In parallel, the source voltage is the same over the components/branches in the supplied lesson.
Year 9 Electricity + Voltage in Series/Parallel

💡 Reflection

  • The normal is a line at 90° to the reflective surface.
  • The incident ray travels towards the surface; the reflected ray travels away.
  • Law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
  • Smooth, shiny surfaces give clear reflection; rough, dull surfaces give diffuse reflection.
Reflection

🔦 Refraction

  • Refraction is the bending of light as it moves between different substances.
  • The bending happens because the speed of light changes in different materials.
  • Entering a more dense medium slows light and, in the supplied lesson, bends it towards the normal.
  • Moving into a less dense medium speeds light up and bends it away from the normal.
  • Refraction explains effects such as a pencil appearing bent in water.
Refraction

🧬 Reproduction

  • Sexual reproduction uses gametes and fertilisation, producing genetically varied offspring.
  • Asexual reproduction does not use sex cells; offspring are generally genetically identical to the parent unless a mutation occurs.
  • Sexual reproduction can increase variation and adaptation, while asexual reproduction can be fast, energy efficient and useful when a mate is unavailable.
  • Gametes are haploid. In humans, body cells contain 46 chromosomes and gametes contain 23.
  • Fertilisation combines gametes to form a zygote, which develops into an embryo.
  • Internal fertilisation occurs inside the body; external fertilisation occurs outside the body and is common in aquatic environments.
  • Viviparous animals develop embryos inside the female and give birth to live young; oviparous animals lay eggs; ovoviviparous animals retain eggs inside the body until they hatch.
  • Asexual strategies in the supplied materials include binary fission, budding, fragmentation, vegetative propagation, spore formation and parthenogenesis.
  • Binary fission involves a single organism dividing into two. The supplied milk scenario uses one bacterium dividing every 20 minutes under ideal conditions.
  • Reproductive strategies can include parental care, and the level of parental care can relate to the number of offspring.

🌊 Great Barrier Reef — Science as a Human Endeavour

  • The supplied task asks how society's values and needs influence the focus of scientific research on the Great Barrier Reef.
  • Listed influences include environmental priorities such as biodiversity conservation and ecosystem services, economic priorities such as tourism and fisheries, social priorities such as community identity and education, and technological priorities such as reef restoration tools and monitoring systems.
  • The task links this work to scientific knowledge being validated and refined through publication and peer review.
  • The task requires a Claim, Evidence and Reasoning paragraph and an annotated bibliography using relevant and reliable sources.
